These headphones fight sound with sound. A jet engine roars, and you barely hear it.

Tiny microphones on each ear cup listen to the noise outside. A chip flips that wave upside down, so every peak becomes a valley, and plays it through a speaker into your ear.

When the waves meet, peak plus valley adds up to flat. Engineers call it destructive interference.

It works best on steady, low rumbles, like engines and ventilation, because they repeat and the chip can predict them. A sudden clap changes too fast, so the cushions have to block it instead.

That's the mechanism. So the quiet you hear is sound fighting sound.
