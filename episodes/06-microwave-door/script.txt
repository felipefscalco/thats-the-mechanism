You can see straight through this microwave door, so why do the microwaves stay in?

Look closer. Behind the glass is a metal screen, full of tiny holes.

The oven runs at two point four five gigahertz. Each wave is about twelve centimetres long.

To a wave that long, those tiny holes might as well be solid metal. It bounces off, back into your food.

Light is different. Its waves are far smaller than the holes, so they slip right through.

What little escapes is capped by US law: five milliwatts per square centimetre, two inches from the oven.

Same door. The microwave sees a wall. Light sees a window.

That's the mechanism. A wall for microwaves, a window for you.
