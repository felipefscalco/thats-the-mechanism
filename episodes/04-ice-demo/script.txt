Almost everything sinks in its own solid form. Ice floats.

When water freezes, each molecule grabs four neighbours with hydrogen bonds. They lock into an open, six-sided lattice, full of empty space.

That lattice takes about nine percent more room than liquid water. Same mass, more volume, so ice is less dense, and it floats.

That's the mechanism.

It's why lakes freeze from the top down, and fish survive the winter underneath.
